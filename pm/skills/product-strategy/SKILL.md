# Product Strategy Canvas — Autostay

## Metadata
- **Name**: product-strategy
- **Description**: Generate a comprehensive product strategy for Autostay using the 9-section Product Strategy Canvas. Covers vision, market segments, costs, value propositions, trade-offs, metrics, growth, capabilities, and defensibility — specialized for O2O car wash subscription.
- **Triggers**: product strategy, strategy canvas, strategic plan, product strategy document

### Domain Context

**Autostay** — O2O car wash subscription service
- Business model: Monthly/annual subscriptions for car wash services
- Customers: Vehicle owners (subscribers)
- Supply: Partner car wash network
- Key metrics: MRR, subscriber count, Churn Rate, LTV, NPS
- O2O loop: Online booking -> Offline car wash -> Digital feedback loop

## Instructions

You are an experienced product strategist developing a comprehensive product strategy for Autostay's O2O car wash subscription service. Apply the context of **$ARGUMENTS**.

Your task is to create a detailed Product Strategy Canvas that outlines how Autostay will compete, win, and grow in the O2O car wash subscription market.

## Input Requirements
- Product description and current positioning
- Market context, competitors, and customer insights
- Company resources, constraints, and priorities
- Any relevant business or market data

## Product Strategy Canvas Template

### 1. Vision
- How can we inspire people?
- What are we aspiring to achieve?
- What values do we uphold?
- How does Autostay transform the car care experience for Korean vehicle owners?

### 2. Market Segments
- Market defined by people's problems (not demographics)
- Jobs to Be Done (JTBD), desired outcomes, constraints
- Who is our first segment?
- Why this segment first?
- Consider: busy professionals, multi-car households, premium car owners, fleet managers, apartment complex residents

### 3. Relative Costs
- Do we optimize for low cost (like budget car wash chains)?
- Or do we emphasize unique value (like premium detailing services)?
- What's our cost position relative to competitors?
- Per-wash unit economics: cost to deliver vs subscription revenue per wash

### 4. Value Proposition
For each target segment:
- **What before**: The customer's current situation, pain, or need (e.g., wasting weekends at car wash, inconsistent quality, weather-dependent scheduling)
- **How**: How Autostay delivers the solution (subscription model, partner network, app-based booking)
- **What after**: The improved outcome or future state (always-clean car, time saved, hassle-free maintenance)
- **Alternatives**: What customers use today instead (self-wash, drive-through, ad-hoc mobile wash)

### 5. Trade-offs
- What will we NOT do?
- What features or markets are out of scope?
- How does saying "no" create focus and amplify our value?
- Consider: own car wash facilities vs partner network, B2C vs B2B, single city vs nationwide

### 6. Key Metrics
- **North Star Metric**: Single metric that drives overall business success (e.g., monthly active subscribers completing washes)
- **OMTM (One Metric That Matters)**: The one metric we optimize for this quarter
- **Subscription metrics**: MRR, Churn Rate, LTV, CAC, LTV/CAC ratio
- **O2O metrics**: Booking completion rate, partner utilization rate, NPS

### 7. Growth
- Sales-Led Growth or Product-Led Growth?
- Primary acquisition channels (app store, referrals, local partnerships, car communities)
- How do we scale? City-by-city expansion
- What's our unit economics?
- O2O flywheel: more subscribers -> more partner demand -> better coverage -> more subscribers

### 8. Capabilities
- What competencies and resources do we need?
- What do we build vs. partner for?
- What capabilities must we develop to win?
- Consider: partner management platform, booking/scheduling system, quality assurance, customer app

### 9. Can't/Won't
- Why can't competitors easily copy this?
- What defensibility do we have (partner network density, switching costs, subscriber data)?
- What barriers to entry exist for new competitors?
- Network effects: geographic density of partners creates convenience moat

## O2O-Specific Strategy Sections

### Partner Network Strategy
- Partner recruitment criteria and onboarding process
- Quality standards and certification requirements
- Revenue share model and partner incentives
- Partner density targets by region (minimum viable supply density)
- Partner retention and satisfaction metrics
- Exclusive vs non-exclusive partnership terms

### Geographic Expansion Strategy
- City selection criteria: vehicle density, car wash market size, partner availability
- Minimum viable supply density per district/neighborhood
- Launch sequence and rollout timeline
- Localization considerations (parking patterns, weather, car culture)
- Hub-and-spoke expansion model within metro areas

### Subscription Model Design
- Tier structure: Basic / Standard / Premium
- Monthly vs annual pricing and discount strategy
- Per-wash allocation vs unlimited models
- Add-on services: interior cleaning, detailing, coating, seasonal packages
- Family/multi-car plans
- Corporate/fleet subscription tiers
- Free trial and onboarding conversion funnel

## Output Process
1. Define the vision and aspirational impact for Autostay
2. Identify 2-3 target market segments with their JTBD
3. Establish cost positioning (low cost vs. premium value)
4. Develop value propositions for each segment
5. List explicit trade-offs (what we won't do)
6. Set North Star and quarterly OMTM with subscription-specific metrics
7. Outline growth strategy and O2O channels
8. Document required capabilities and partnerships
9. Explain defensibility and barriers to competition
10. Define partner network strategy
11. Outline geographic expansion approach
12. Design subscription model and tier structure
13. Validate strategy coherence: ensure elements reinforce each other
14. Surface critical hypotheses that must be true for success
15. Suggest low-effort experiments to test key assumptions

## Notes
- Ensure all 9 elements plus O2O-specific sections fit together logically
- Identify what must be true for this strategy to work (hypotheses)
- Propose validation experiments with minimal effort
- Strategy guides decisions; clarity enables faster execution
- Revisit quarterly as market conditions change
- For O2O businesses, supply-side strategy (partner network) is as important as demand-side strategy (subscribers)
